I lived in Stockholm for almost eight months, from November 2025 to July 2026. It was a
relatively short time, but somehow I left with so many long-lasting memories. One of them was
the day Mälaren froze over.

Last winter, I walked across the frozen lake, and it felt almost surreal to see a place that is
usually so alive with water and boats transformed into a giant winter playground.

{% include photo.html src="/images/travel/stockholm/city-hall.jpg" caption="Standing on the ice in front of Stockholm City Hall." %}

{% include photo.html src="/images/travel/stockholm/frozen-ship.jpg" caption="This ship wasn't going anywhere that day." %}

People were walking their dogs, strolling with their babies, and spending time on the ice as if
they were having a picnic in the park. Some were simply wandering around, enjoying the winter sun
and the strange feeling of being able to walk across a frozen lake.

<div class="photo-row">
  {% include photo.html src="/images/travel/stockholm/frozen-boats.jpg" caption="Boats frozen in place, with footprints all around them." %}
  {% include photo.html src="/images/travel/stockholm/cracked-ice.jpg" caption="Cracked ice near the shore, and the city just across it." %}
</div>

It was one of those quiet Stockholm moments that stayed with me. The city, the frozen lake, and
everyone casually enjoying it together.
